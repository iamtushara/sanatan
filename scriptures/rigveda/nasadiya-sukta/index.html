MANTRA01

Neither Being Nor Non-Being

नासदासीन्नो सदासीत्तदानीम्
nāsad āsīn no sad āsīt tadānīm
नासीद्रजो नो व्योमा परो यत्
nāsīd rajo no vyomā paro yat
किमावरीवः कुह कस्य शर्मन्
kim āvarīvaḥ kuha kasya śarman
अम्भः किमासीद्गहनं गभीरम् ॥१॥
ambhaḥ kim āsīd gahanaṃ gabhīram
Translation

Then, neither non-being nor being was;

neither the mid-region nor the heaven beyond it was.

What covered [all this]? Where? In whose shelter?

Was there water, deep and unfathomable?

Meaning and interpretation

The opening does not merely say “there was nothing.” It suspends both sat (being) and asat (non-being), challenging the categories through which we usually describe reality. The verse also asks about space, shelter and primordial water. These are questions, not a settled account of what physically existed.

Words and meanings
IAST / SanskritMeaning and grammatical note
न / na“not”; indeclinable negation.
असत् / asat“non-being, what is not”; neuter form used substantively. In nāsat, na + asat is joined by sandhi.
आसीत् / āsīt“was, existed”; third-person singular imperfect of √as, “to be.”
नो / no“nor”; a Vedic form or combination commonly explained as na u in this context.
सत् / sat“being, what is”; present participial form of √as, used as a noun.
तदानीम् / tadānīm“then, at that time”; temporal adverb.
रजस् / rajas“atmosphere, mid-region” in this context; neuter noun.
व्योमन् / vyoman“sky, heaven, expanse”; here vyomā is the form in the verse.
परः / paraḥ“beyond, farther”; adjective/adverbial form describing what lies beyond the sky.
किम् / kim“what?”; interrogative pronoun, neuter singular.
आवरीवः / āvarīvaḥ“covered, enveloped”; a difficult Vedic form whose precise grammatical analysis and meaning remain uncertain, so translations vary.
कुह / kuha“where?”; interrogative adverb.
कस्य / kasya“whose?”; genitive singular of ka, “who?”
शर्मन् / śarman“shelter, protection, secure place”; locative sense in the question “in whose shelter?”
अम्भस् / ambhas“water”; neuter noun.
गहनम् / gahanam“deep, impenetrable”; neuter form agreeing with the implied depth of the water.
गभीरम् / gabhīram“profound, unfathomable”; neuter adjective.

Grammar to Note

Parsing the opening: nāsat is best unpacked as na + asat (“not non-being”), while nāsīt is na + āsīt (“was not”). In nāsad āsīt, the final consonant of asat changes at the word boundary; this is sandhi, not a separate word. The repeated negative construction is parallel rather than a single compound sentence.

Questions and cases: kim is the neuter interrogative “what?”; kuha asks “where?”; kasya is genitive singular, “whose?”; and śarman is interpreted locatively, “in shelter/protection.” ambhaḥ is a neuter nominative form, “water,” followed by the adjectives gahanam and gabhīram (“deep, unfathomable”). The exact construction around āvarīvaḥ is uncertain in the transmitted Vedic wording, so translations should not pretend its syntax is completely settled.

MANTRA02

The One, Without Ordinary Breath

न मृत्युरासीदमृतं न तर्हि
na mṛtyur āsīd amṛtaṃ na tarhi
न रात्र्या अह्न आसीत्प्रकेतः
na rātryā ahna āsīt praketaḥ
आनीदवातं स्वधया तदेकम्
ānīd avātaṃ svadhayā tad ekam
तस्माद्धान्यन्न परः किंचनास ॥२॥
tasmād dhānyan na paraḥ kiñcanāsa
Translation

There was neither death nor immortality then.

There was no distinguishing sign of night or day.

That One breathed, windless, by its own power;

apart from it, nothing else was.

Meaning and interpretation

Ordinary time is tracked by cycles and contrasts: day/night, life/death. The verse imagines a condition in which these distinctions do not yet operate. “That One” is self-sustaining, described paradoxically as breathing without wind. Calling it “pure consciousness” or identifying it with a later theological concept is an interpretation, not an explicit definition in this verse.

Words and meanings

मृत्यु / mṛtyu“death”; masculine noun, here in a sandhi-joined form before āsīt.
अमृतम् / amṛtam“deathlessness, immortality”; literally “not-death”; neuter noun or adjective.
तर्हि / tarhi“then, at that time”; adverb.
रात्र्याः / rātryāḥ“of night”; genitive singular, paired with ahnaḥ (“of day”) in the phrase denying a marker that separates night from day.
अह्नः / ahnaḥ“of day”; genitive singular of the Vedic noun ahan, with an irregular stem.
प्रकेतः / praketaḥ“sign, indication, distinguishing mark”; the line denies a marker separating night from day.
आनीत् / ānīt“breathed, stirred”; Vedic verb form, traditionally interpreted here as breathing.
अवातम् / avātam“without wind/air”; formed with privative a- + vāta, “wind.”
स्वधया / svadhayā“by its own power/nature”; instrumental singular of svadhā.
तदेकम् / tad ekam“that One”; demonstrative tad + numeral/adjective ekam, neuter singular.
तस्मात् / tasmāt“from that, apart from that”; ablative singular of tad.
अन्यत् / anyat“another, anything else”; neuter form, appearing in a sandhi-rich phrase.
किम् चन / kiṃ cana“anything whatsoever”; kim (“what?”) plus the indefinite particle cana, used with negation.
आस / ās(a)“was”; Vedic verbal form in the final phrase.

Grammar to Note

Negation and parallel nouns: na mṛtyuḥ … amṛtam na pairs “death” and “immortality”; the verse denies both, rather than saying that one is hidden inside the other. The form praketaḥ means a sign or distinguishing indication, and the syntax connects it with the absence of a marker separating night from day.

Instrumental and subject: svadhayā is instrumental singular: “by its own power/nature.” It answers “by what means?” tad ekam (“that One”) is neuter singular and is understood as the subject of the breathing/stirring expression. avātam describes it as “windless” or “without air.” The phrase is intentionally paradoxical: breathing is described where ordinary breath and wind are absent. The final pāda is compressed Vedic syntax; the translation “apart from that, nothing else was” conveys the common reading without claiming that every sandhi boundary is straightforward.

MANTRA03

Darkness Concealed by Darkness

तम आसीत्तमसा गूळ्हमग्रे
tama āsīt tamasā gūḷham agre
अप्रकेतं सलिलं सर्वमा इदम्
apraketaṃ salilaṃ sarvam ā idam
तुच्छ्येनाभ्वपिहितं यदासीत्
tuchyenābhv apihitaṃ yad āsīt
तपसस्तन्महिनाजायतैकम् ॥३॥
tapasas tan mahinājāyataikam
Translation

Darkness was hidden by darkness in the beginning;

all this was an indistinguishable flood.

That which was enclosed by emptiness, unformed,

came into being through the power of tapas.

Meaning and interpretation

The verse gives images rather than a physical model: darkness, an unmarked watery expanse, concealment and tapas. “Heat” can suggest generative intensity, while “fervour” can suggest concentrated power. It is tempting to equate this directly with the Big Bang, but the hymn does not state a modern scientific theory.

तमस् / tamas“darkness”; neuter noun. tama āsīt is the verse’s “darkness was.”
तमसा / tamasā“by/with darkness”; instrumental singular, marking what conceals.
गूळ्हम् / gūḷham“hidden, concealed”; past participial form used adjectivally.
अग्रे / agre“at first, in the beginning”; locative form used adverbially.
अप्रकेतम् / apraketam“unmarked, indistinguishable, not manifest”; privative a- + praketa.
सलिलम् / salilam“water, flood, fluid expanse”; neuter noun.
सर्वम् इदम् / sarvam idam“all this”; neuter singular adjective/pronoun phrase.
तुच्छ्येन / tuchyena“by emptiness/void”; the exact nuance is debated among translators.
आभु / ābhuA difficult Vedic term often understood as an unformed or nascent state; interpretations vary.
अपिहितम् / apihitam“covered, enclosed”; past participle from a verb meaning “to cover” or “close over.”
तपस् / tapas“heat, fervour, ardour, creative intensity”; tapasaḥ is genitive in the phrase “through/by the power of tapas.”
महिना / mahinā“by greatness/power”; instrumental singular of mahimā.

Grammar to Note

Instrumental of means: tamasā is instrumental singular of tamas (“darkness”), so the phrase means “hidden/covered by darkness.” gūḷham is a participial adjective, “concealed,” and relates to the thing being hidden. agre is an adverbial locative, “at first / in the beginning.”

Adjectives and the emergence verb: apraketam (“unmarked, indistinguishable”) and salilam (“water/flood”) are neuter forms describing the undifferentiated state. The words in the third pāda are notoriously difficult: tuchya, ābhu and the compound-like apihitam have been interpreted differently. ajāyata means “was born/came into being,” and ekam is the neuter “One” that emerges. tapasaḥ … mahinā is commonly rendered “through/by the greatness or power of tapas”; tapas may mean heat, ardour or concentrated creative fervour, not necessarily literal temperature alone.

MANTRA04

Desire, the First Seed of Mind

कामस्तदग्रे समवर्तताधि
kāmas tad agre sam avartatādhi
मनसो रेतः प्रथमं यदासीत्
manaso retaḥ prathamaṃ yad āsīt
सतो बन्धुमसति निरविन्दन्
sato bandhum asati nir avindan
हृदि प्रतीष्या कवयो मनीषा ॥४॥
hṛdi pratīṣyā kavayo manīṣā
Translation

Desire arose in the beginning;

it was the first seed of mind.

The seers discovered the bond of being in non-being,

searching in their hearts with insight.

Meaning and interpretation

Kāma here need not mean ordinary personal or sensual desire. It can mean the first inclination toward manifestation—the impulse from which thought or differentiation begins. The sages then discern a “bond” between being and non-being. The verse offers a speculative insight, not a fully elaborated metaphysical system.

तद् अग्रे / tad agre“then, in the beginning”; demonstrative plus temporal expression.
समवर्तत / sam-avartata“arose, came into activity”; middle-voice verbal form of √vṛt, “turn, occur, become active.”
अधि / adhiA preverb or particle whose exact attachment here is debated; translations often render the phrase simply as “arose in the beginning.”
मनसः / manasaḥ“of mind”; genitive singular of neuter manas.
रेतस् / retaḥ“seed, seminal essence”; neuter noun, metaphorically the first germ.
प्रथमम् / prathamam“first”; neuter singular, agreeing with retaḥ.
यत् / yat“which, that”; relative pronoun linking the clause.
सतः / sataḥ“of what exists, of being”; genitive singular of sat.
बन्धुम् / bandhum“bond, connection, relation”; accusative singular, object of “found.”

Grammar to Note

Subject and verb: kāmaḥ (“desire”) is nominative singular and the subject of sam-avartata (“arose, came into activity”). The verbal form is middle voice, a common way of presenting something as coming about or becoming active. tad agre situates this “in the beginning,” while the particle adhi is difficult to attach precisely and is handled differently by translators.

Genitive, locative and plural verb: manasaḥ is genitive singular (“of mind”); retaḥ prathamam means “the first seed,” with neuter prathamam agreeing with retaḥ. sataḥ is genitive (“of being”), whereas asati is locative (“in non-being”). bandhum, accusative singular, is the object of nir-avindan, “they found.” The plural subject is the kavayaḥ, “seers”; hṛdi is locative (“in the heart”), and manīṣā conveys the instrument or manner, “with insight/wisdom.” The last phrase is compressed, so “searching in their hearts with insight” is a reasonable syntactic expansion rather than a literal word order.

MANTRA05

Was There Something Above or Below?

तिरश्चीनो विततो रश्मिरेषाम्
tiraścīno vitato raśmir eṣām
अधः स्विदासीदुपरि स्विदासीत्
adhaḥ svid āsīd upari svid āsīt
रेतोधा आसन्महिमान आसन्
retodhā āsan mahimāna āsan
स्वधा अवस्तात्प्रयतिः परस्तात् ॥५॥
svadhā avastāt prayatiḥ parastāt
Translation

Their line was stretched across.

Was there something below? Was there something above?

There were seed-bearers; there were mighty powers.

Self-sustaining potency was below, and impulse above.

Meaning and interpretation

The hymn now asks how the first distinctions or relationships might be conceived: a line across, above and below, seed-bearing forces and powers. Some translators read this as the beginnings of cosmic structure; others retain the deliberately obscure imagery. It should not be treated as a clear technical diagram of space or matter.

Words and meanings

तिरश्चीनः / tiraścīnaḥ“crosswise, horizontally”; adjective/adverbial form describing the line or ray.
विततः / vitataḥ“stretched out, extended”; past participle from vi-√tan, “spread.”
रश्मिः / raśmiḥ“ray, cord, line”; masculine nominative singular.
एषाम् / eṣām“of these, their”; genitive plural pronoun.
अधः / adhaḥ“below”; adverb.
स्विद् / svidAn interrogative or emphatic particle, here giving the sense “perhaps?” or “indeed?” in the repeated questions.
उपरि / upari“above”; adverb.
आसीत् / āsīt“was”; third-person singular imperfect of √as.
रेतोधाः / retodhāḥ“seed-placers, seed-bearers”; plural agent noun related to retas, “seed.” Exact cosmological referent is uncertain.
आसन् / āsan“they were”; third-person plural imperfect of √as.
महिमानः / mahimānaḥ“greatnesses, mighty powers”; masculine nominative plural.
स्वधा / svadhā“inherent power, self-sustaining nature”; noun with rich ritual and philosophical associations.
अवस्तात् / avastāt“below, on the lower side”; adverb.

Grammar to Note

Agreement and number: raśmiḥ (“line/ray/cord”) is nominative singular, described by tiraścīnaḥ (“crosswise”) and vitataḥ (“stretched out”). eṣām is genitive plural, “of these/their.” In the questions, adhaḥ and upari mean “below” and “above”; svid is a Vedic particle adding an exploratory or emphatic force, not a noun to translate independently.

Plural clauses and verbless contrast: āsan is third-person plural imperfect (“they were”), unlike singular āsīt (“it was”). retodhāḥ and mahimānaḥ are plural nominative expressions: approximately “seed-bearers” and “great powers.” The last pāda has no explicit finite verb: svadhā avastāt, prayatiḥ parastāt—“inherent power below; impulse/exertion above.” Because svadhā, retodhāḥ and prayatiḥ are semantically rich Vedic terms, a precise physical interpretation is less certain than the basic grammatical contrasts.

MANTRA06

Who Truly Knows?

को अद्धा वेद क इह प्र वोचत्
ko addhā veda ka iha pra vocat
कुत आजाता कुत इयं विसृष्टिः
kuta ājātā kuta iyaṃ visṛṣṭiḥ
अर्वाग्देवा अस्य विसर्जनेनाथा
arvāg devā asya visarjanena athā
को वेद यत आबभूव ॥६॥
ko veda yata ābabhūva
Translation

Who knows it for certain? Who here can declare it?

From where was it born? From where did this creation arise?

The gods came after this world's coming-forth;

so who knows from where it arose?

Meaning and interpretation

This is the hymn’s explicit epistemological turn. If the gods themselves came after the world’s coming-forth, they cannot straightforwardly be witnesses to its first cause. The verse asks who can speak with certainty about an origin that precedes the knowers and the order they inhabit.

वेद / veda“knows”; perfect form of √vid, “know,” with present meaning.
इह / iha“here, in this world”; adverb.
प्र वोचत् / pra vocat“might declare, speak forth”; a Vedic verbal form from √vac, “speak.”
कुतः / kutaḥ“from where?”; interrogative adverb.
आजाता / ājātā“born, arisen”; feminine nominative singular past participle, agreeing with visṛṣṭiḥ.
इयम् / iyam“this”; feminine nominative singular, agreeing with visṛṣṭiḥ.
विसृष्टिः / visṛṣṭiḥ“emission, creation, bringing-forth”; feminine noun.

Grammar to Note

Interrogative pattern: kaḥ (“who?”) and kutaḥ (“from where?”) frame repeated questions. addhā intensifies the demand for reliable knowledge (“truly, for certain”), and iha means “here.” veda is formally a perfect form of √vid, but has present meaning: “knows.” pra vocat uses √vac (“speak”) with the preverb pra, “speak forth / declare.”

Creation as grammatical subject: iyam visṛṣṭiḥ is “this creation,” both feminine nominative singular; ājātā is the feminine verbal form (“was born/arose”). The repeated kutaḥ asks for the source twice, intensifying the question. In arvāg devāḥ, devāḥ is nominative plural and arvāk conveys “later than / subsequent to” in this context. asya visarjanena is an instrumental phrase, “at/by this creation’s sending forth.” The concluding ko veda yata ābabhūva asks who knows the source from which it came to be.

MANTRA07

Or Perhaps Even the Highest Does Not Know

इयं विसृष्टिर्यत आबभूव
iyaṃ visṛṣṭir yata ābabhūva
यदि वा दधे यदि वा न
yadi vā dadhe yadi vā na
यो अस्याध्यक्षः परमे व्योमन्
yo asyādhyakṣaḥ parame vyoman
सो अङ्ग वेद यदि वा न वेद ॥७॥
so aṅga veda yadi vā na veda
Translation

This creation—whence it came into being,

whether it was established or whether it was not—

he who is its overseer in the highest heaven,

he surely knows—or perhaps he does not know.

Meaning and interpretation

The final verse does not simply conclude that no creator exists. It asks whether the highest overseer knows the origin, and then leaves open the possibility that even this being does not know. The ending is striking because it allows uncertainty to remain at the summit of the inquiry itself.

Words and meanings

इयम् / iyam“this”; feminine nominative singular, agreeing with visṛṣṭiḥ.
विसृष्टिः / visṛṣṭiḥ“creation, emission, bringing-forth”; feminine noun.
यतः / yataḥ“from where, whence”; adverb introducing the question about the source of creation. The verse’s yata ābabhūva reflects the Vedic sandhi at the word boundary.
आबभूव / ābabhūva“came into being”; perfect of √bhū.
यदि वा … यदि वा न / yadi vā … yadi vā na“whether … or whether not”; paired conditional expressions.
दधे / dadhe“placed, established, formed”; middle-voice perfect form of √dhā, “put, place.” The object is implicit in the context.
यः / yaḥ“who, he who”; masculine nominative singular relative pronoun.
अस्य / asya“of this”; genitive singular.
अध्यक्षः / adhyakṣaḥ“overseer, supervisor, one who presides”; masculine nominative singular.
परमे / parame“highest, supreme”; locative singular agreeing with vyoman.
व्योमन् / vyoman“sky, heaven, expanse”; locative singular in “in the highest heaven.”
सः / saḥ“he”; masculine nominative singular demonstrative, joined with the following vowel by sandhi.
अङ्ग / aṅgaEmphatic particle: “indeed, surely”; it can also serve as a discourse particle.

Grammar to Note

Relative-correlative construction: yaḥ … saḥ means “he who … he.” adhyakṣaḥ is the masculine nominative subject, “overseer”; asya is genitive, “of this [creation].” parame vyoman is locative singular: “in the highest heaven/expanse,” with parame agreeing with vyoman.

Alternatives and emphasis: yadi vā … yadi vā na means “whether … or whether not,” leaving the status of creation open. dadhe is a middle-voice perfect form of √dhā (“put, place, establish”); translators differ on whether to render it “was formed,” “was sustained,” or “was established.” veda again means “knows,” despite its perfect morphology. aṅga adds emphasis (“indeed / surely”), but the final yadi vā na veda immediately qualifies the certainty: “or perhaps he does not know.” This balance is the verse’s central grammatical and philosophical effect.

The final question
“He knows—or perhaps he does not know.”

The Nāsadīya Sūkta offers no easy closure. Its lasting philosophical force lies in asking what can be known about the origin of everything, and in refusing to turn a difficult question into false certainty.

After the seventh verse

What kind of answer does the hymn give?

The Nāsadīya Sūkta offers images and relationships, but not a confident report from an eyewitness. Its claims, questions, and later readers’ explanations should be kept distinct. Several enduring debates grow from that difference.

  1. 01

    Cosmogony, or inquiry into knowledge?

    The hymn narrates emergence, then asks who could know its source. It can be read both as cosmological poetry and as a reflection on the limits of cosmological claims.

  2. 02

    Who—or what—is “that One”?

    Tad ekam in verse 2 is grammatically neuter and unnamed. A reading in terms of divine unity is possible, but identifying it outright with a later doctrine goes beyond the phrase alone.

  3. 03

    Does uncertainty mean disbelief?

    The poem questions the knowability of beginnings and the gods’ place in their telling. That epistemic hesitation is not, by itself, a denial of the gods.

  4. 04

    What does the last line leave open?

    The overseer in the highest heaven may know—or may not. The ending preserves this unresolved question as an invitation to thought; later theological readings should not replace the wording of the verse.

  5. 05

    Is this a description of modern cosmology?

    No. The Big Bang, quantum vacuum, and modern physical energy are contemporary comparisons, not translations of this ancient poem. Similar-sounding images do not establish a scientific prediction.

Traditional reception
The traditional index names Prajāpati Parameṣṭhin as the seer and classifies the metre as Triṣṭubh. These are valuable facts about the hymn’s transmission. Later commentarial readings—including Sāyaṇa’s theological reading—belong to the history of its interpretation; they should not be quietly substituted for the poem’s earlier, more open language.

Textual and linguistic note: This page is a study aid, not a critical edition or a traditional bhāṣya. The Devanagari is displayed without Vedic svara accents, and the line-by-line IAST is a readable study transliteration. Vedic morphology and several words in this hymn are debated; notes flag especially uncertain readings rather than presenting every gloss as definitive.
