Nāsadīya Sūkta

The hymn of creation · Ṛgveda 10.129

नासदासीन्नो सदासीत्तदानीं नासीद्रजो नो व्योमा परो यत् ।
किमावरीवः कुह कस्य शर्मन्नम्भः किमासीद्गहनं गभीरम् ॥१॥

nāsad āsīn no sad āsīt tadānīṃ nāsīd rajo no vyomā paro yat |
kim āvarīvaḥ kuha kasya śarmann ambhaḥ kim āsīd gahanaṃ gabhīram || 1 ||

Then, there was neither non-existence nor existence; there was no realm of air, nor the sky beyond it. What covered it, and where? In whose protection? Was there water, unfathomably deep?

What makes this hymn distinctive?

The Nāsadīya Sūkta is a creation hymn from the tenth maṇḍala of the Ṛgveda. Rather than presenting a simple account of creation, it examines the limits of what can be known about the beginning of the cosmos. Its opening questions the applicability of familiar categories such as existence, non-existence, space, and water to a state before the ordered world.

The hymn proceeds through images of darkness, the undifferentiated state, desire, and the emergence of a creative principle. It culminates not in an entirely certain answer, but in a striking question about whether anyone—even the highest overseer—can know how creation arose.

Central themes

1. The limits of ordinary categories

The opening verse challenges the reader to imagine a beginning before the distinctions by which the world is normally described. Even the contrast between being and non-being is placed under scrutiny.

2. Emergence and desire

In the fourth verse, desire (kāma) is described as an early seed of mind. This has inspired philosophical reflection, but the hymn should not be treated as if it were a modern scientific theory.

3. Uncertainty as a conclusion

The final verse asks whether the origin is known at all. The hymn's intellectual force lies partly in its willingness to leave the question open.
